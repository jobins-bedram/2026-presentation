Chapter 2 · 2023

The ¥60 Resume

The moment I started becoming an AI engineer

At 2023, we used to use Daxtra as service, and it costs around ¥60 to parse every single resume.

Before the Spark

  • Every resume that entered JoBins had to be parsed into structured data
  • We relied on Dextra , a third-party parsing service, to do it
  • Price: ~¥60 per resume — every single one
  • The cost scaled linearly with our growth: more candidates, bigger bill
  • I didn't control the quality, the format, or the roadmap — someone else did
The better we did as a business, the more we paid someone else.
Every resume entering JoBins had to become structured data. The cost scaled with our growth, and we controlled nothing: not the quality, not the format, not the roadmap. The better we did, the more we paid someone else.

The Spark — Early 2023

  • Then OpenAI released GPT-3.5 ( gpt-3.5-turbo ) — API access at $0.002 / 1K tokens
  • I couldn't stop thinking about one question:
"Can a general-purpose LLM parse a resume as well as a purpose-built service — at a fraction of the cost?"
  • So I gave myself an afternoon to find out — and built a basic prototype
  • It parsed quite accurately — even Japanese resumes
Then OpenAI released GPT-3.5 with API access at $0.002 per thousand tokens. I couldn't stop thinking: could a general-purpose LLM match a purpose-built parsing service? So I gave myself one afternoon and built a basic prototype. And it parsed accurately — even Japanese resumes.

The Doubt — and the Challenge

  • Still, I wasn't sure. Building a prototype was one thing — integrating it into production was another
  • Then one day, Sazn dai gave me the challenge: build resume parsing — for production.
  • My team and I started building. The problems we'd worried about? We solved them
  • We replaced Dextra in candidate management — and from there it spread: job management, selection management, and later AICA as well
But honestly — I wasn't sure. A prototype is one thing; production is another. Then Sazn dai gave me the challenge: build it for real. So my team and I did. We solved the problems we'd been worried about, and successful replacing the Dextra in candidate management — and from there it spread to job management, selection management, and later AICA.

The Math — Cost per Resume/Business Impact

Side-by-side against Dextra on real resumes: comparably good parsing quality — no fine-tuning, no new infrastructure, just a well-designed prompt.

Dextra GPT-3.5
Pricing model flat fee per resume $0.002 per 1K tokens
Usage per resume 1 parse ~5,000 tokens
Cost (USD) — 5 × $0.002 = $0.010
Cost (JPY @ ¥140/USD) ¥60.0 ¥1.4

~98% cheaper

(1 − 1.4 / 60 = 97.7% cost reduction )

So what was the business impact? If we consider one resume, it takes around 5,000 tokens to parse, which costs about one cent — around ¥1.4. With Dextra, the same resume was costing us ¥60. That is almost 98% cheaper. And the parsing quality was comparably good — no fine-tuning, no new infrastructure, just a well-designed prompt.
